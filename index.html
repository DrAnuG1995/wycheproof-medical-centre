Getting help

After hours and emergencies

The clinic is open Monday to Friday. This page is what to do the rest of the time, and what to do at any hour if something is serious.

Any hour, any day

Call 000

Chest pain. Trouble breathing. Signs of a stroke, meaning face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech. Serious injury. Bleeding that will not stop. Someone unconscious.

Do not drive to the clinic. Do not wait to see if it settles. If you are not sure how sick you are, ring 000 and let them decide. Nobody will mind.

Our services

A general practice in a town this size has to do a bit of everything. Here is what that means in practice.

Everyday general practice

Coughs and colds, infections, injuries, skin problems, aches and pains, contraception, sexual health, sleep, fatigue: the things people actually come in for.

Long-term conditions

Diabetes, heart disease, blood pressure, asthma and COPD, kidney disease, arthritis. Care plans, reviews, and coordination with your specialists.

Mental health

Mental health treatment plans, review, and referral. We know the local services and how long each of them actually takes.

Skin checks

A general skin examination is booked for a Wednesday morning. If anything needs removing, that is a separate booking the following Wednesday.

  • General skin examination, Wednesday mornings
  • Minor skin lesion excision, every second Wednesday morning
  • Liquid nitrogen cryotherapy for corns, callus and warts

Women's health

  • Cervical screening test, previously the Pap smear
  • Implanon
  • Mirena
  • STI screening

Men's health

  • STI screening
  • Subcutaneous and intramuscular hormone injections, with a specialist recommendation

Maternal and child health

Pregnancy care shared with the hospital and your midwife, postnatal checks, and the early childhood checks and immunisations that go with them.

Point of care ultrasound

Bedside ultrasound during your consultation to help sort out what is going on, which can save a trip to a larger centre. It does not replace a formal scan when one is needed.

Children and school-age

Farm and workplace injury

Care for residents in aged care

We provide the medical care for all residents. If you are arranging care for a parent from a distance, this page is for you.

How it works

One of our doctors visits regularly, so residents are seen by someone who knows them rather than whoever happens to be on. Nursing staff contact us directly when something changes, and we see people at the facility rather than moving them.

We register residents in MyMedicare where families agree, which formally links them to this practice and funds the extra care planning and regular review that aged care needs. More about MyMedicare

What we do there

  • Regular review of every resident, not just when something goes wrong
  • Medication review and deprescribing, because most people arrive on more tablets than they need
  • Dementia assessment and behaviour management
  • Advance care planning, with the family in the room
  • End-of-life care at the facility, so nobody has to spend their last week in an ambulance

The honest version

MyMedicare exists because the government wants people to have one regular practice rather than shopping around. That happens to be how general practice works best anyway, and in a town with one clinic it describes what is already true.

What it changes for us is funding, because several Medicare items only apply to registered patients. What it changes for you is that we can offer those services. That is the whole deal.

What it does not do

  • It does not stop you seeing another doctor anywhere, any time
  • It does not cost you anything
  • It does not give us access to anything we do not already have
  • You can cancel it yourself whenever you like

How to register

  1. Mention it at your next appointment, or ring reception.
  2. We start the registration and you confirm it, through your Medicare online account or the Express Plus app, or on a form at the desk if you would rather not use a computer.
  3. That is it. Nothing changes about how you book or what you pay.

How we work

Practice policies

The dull but important page. It is also what an accreditation surveyor opens first, so it is kept current.

Test results

Every result is discussed with you, whether it is normal or abnormal. You will usually be booked a follow up appointment at the end of that week or the end of the following week to go through it.

If a result is urgent

We ring you.

If it is not urgent

We send a text to say your results are ready, not the result itself. If we hear nothing we text twice more, three in total, and then write to you by registered post.

If you have heard nothing

Ring reception after 5 days and ask whether your results are back, then book a follow up. Do not assume no news is good news.

We do not give results by text or email. Reception cannot interpret results and will pass you to the nurse. The nurse can give you viral swab, INR and blood sugar results. If any of those are abnormal, the nurse books you with the doctor to talk about what happens next.

Repeat prescriptions

Ask as soon as you have 5 working days of tablets left, not on the last one. Some medications need a consultation before we can repeat them, which is not us being difficult, it is a condition of prescribing them safely.

Referrals

Referrals need a consultation. We cannot backdate them, and specialists will not accept one that has expired.

How we communicate

We return phone calls within 24 hours. Email and web forms are not monitored for anything clinical or urgent. If it matters today, ring us.

Undifferentiated acute
